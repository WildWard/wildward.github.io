Mission

Technology that works for wildlife, not just about it.

Rangers, researchers and small conservation groups collect huge amounts of recordings and photos, then run out of hours to go through them. We build free, offline AI tools that do that work, starting where the need is clearest and the tools are missing.

Why bats first

The forest's night shift has no one counting it.

Bats pollinate, spread seeds and eat tonnes of insects every night. Southeast Asia is one of the richest bat regions on Earth, and many of its species live nowhere else. Yet across much of the region, scientists still identify bat calls by hand, because no software knows their species.

Project 1 · Southeast Asian bat calls

AI that knows Asia's bats

Today's bat AI was trained on European and American bats. We tested a leading free model on twelve recordings of Malaysian bats from the ChiroVox library, species that also range across Indonesia, Thailand, the Philippines and beyond. It didn't name a single one correctly.

  • Works offline. Runs on a laptop at a field station, no internet needed.
  • Learns local species. Trained on recordings that local experts identified.
  • Open and fair. Free to use. Researchers keep ownership of their data.

Get WildWard ListenWhy it matters: bats & wind

Our free, offline review tool: finds every bat pass, measures every call, groups similar calls and learns from your labels. Before anyone labels anything, it already points to the right bat family 78% of the time (tested on 237 recordings). Early version, runs with Python.

First result · Oct 2026

A bird AI learned 102 Asian bat species from a handful of examples each

We slowed the ultrasonic calls down twelve times, so Google's Perch 2.0, a model trained on birdsong, could hear them. Then we trained a small classifier on top: 888 recordings of 102 species from 85 sites in Vietnam, Malaysia and Indonesia, at most fifteen recordings per species.

77%of recordings named to the right species, out of 102 (95% range 74-80%). Random guessing gets 1%.
90%named to the right genus (87-91%).
63%species right on recording sites the model never heard (59-67%; 82% genus; 49 species recorded at 3+ sites). In a controlled test, training on 4 sites instead of 1, with the same amount of data, added 18 points. More places means better AI. That's where partners come in.

Early results, honest scope: 102 of Southeast Asia's 390+ species, mostly clean close-range recordings from the ChiroVox library, many species with only a few recordings. Real survey recordings are fainter and messier, and species the model has never heard are not handled yet, so field accuracy will be lower until we train on partners' survey data. Recording-level cross-validation; ranges are 95% confidence intervals. Updated 25 Oct 2026. ChiroVox data used locally for non-commercial research. Code, method and caveats: github.com/WildWard/batlab.

Founder

Antal Kiss

"The wild is disappearing faster than the people protecting it can keep up. I want to give them better tools."

I'm a builder with a background in business informatics. I've shipped games, AI assistants and web products, mostly on my own, with AI as my engineering team. That taught me something that changes everything for conservation: one person with the right tools can now do what used to take a whole lab.

So I'm pointing everything I can build at the problem I care about most. WildWard starts with the bats of Southeast Asia, because the gap is clear and the researchers need help now. The goal is much bigger: a toolkit that lets every ranger and researcher, anywhere, see and hear what's happening in their forest.

I build with the people who do the fieldwork, not over their heads. If that's you, I'd love to talk.
